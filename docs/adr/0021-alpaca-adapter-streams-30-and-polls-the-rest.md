# The Alpaca adapter streams 30 symbols and polls the rest

Alpaca's free tier streams at most 30 symbols on one connection. Reference counting removes duplicates but does not raise that cap, so two users at different scroll positions, or a search hit from a 10,000-symbol catalogue, can need a 31st symbol. The Alpaca adapter handles this internally. The 30 most-watched symbols stream, and the rest are refreshed every 5 seconds through Alpaca's multi-symbol snapshot endpoint, one request per batch, about 12 requests a minute against the 200 allowed. Polled quotes flow through the same hub, conflation and timestamps, and symbols move between streamed and polled as watch counts change. Nothing on the wire or in the apps knows the difference. The simulator has no cap.

## Consequences

- The Alpaca ticket must measure the per-call symbol limit and the rate-limit cost of a batch, since neither is documented.
- The hub reports each symbol's watcher count to the live feed through an optional `watchCount` on `LiveFeed`, because subscribe and unsubscribe arrive only at 0 to 1 and 1 to 0. The simulator ignores it. On a tie the streamed symbol keeps its place, so equally watched symbols do not swap back and forth.
- Until the first keyed run measures them, a snapshot batch holds at most 100 symbols and is assumed to cost one request. The verify skill lists what that run measures.
- Every Alpaca market data request (polling, subscribe replies, the hub's retries, session resyncs, missing baselines and history) shares one budget of 180 requests a minute, 10% under Basic's 200. Snapshot requests for a symbol already in flight join that request, and a symbol is not asked again until 5 seconds after its last request finished. A failure pauses every data request for 5 seconds, doubling to 60, and a 429 pauses them for the full minute. Every Alpaca request, trading API included, gives up after 4 seconds. For a data request that counts as a failure and starts the pause, while a caller's own abort does not. A request the budget refuses fails at once without reaching Alpaca, so the hub answers `unavailable` and the history chain falls back. The trading API (clock, calendar, assets) is outside this budget.
- Each held quote records the session it was priced for, as the trading date and whether the market was open. A streamed trade is published only when it falls inside the current session and the held baseline belongs to that session. Otherwise the newest trade waits while its session is current, and the poll keeps retrying the resync under the budget, open or closed, until a fresh baseline arrives. A snapshot that finishes in a later session than it started in is discarded and retried, and the waiting trade survives it. The adapter never serves a quote from an earlier session as the current snapshot.
- During an outage at the open or close, the hub also expires prior-phase quotes. Subscribers waiting for a current quote receive it as a tick on recovery ([ADR 0044](0044-hub-enforces-quote-phase-from-timestamps.md)).
- When a snapshot lacks the close of the session before the one being priced, the adapter asks for that session's daily bar. If the bar cannot be fetched the request fails, rather than showing zero change.
